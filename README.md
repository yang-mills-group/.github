This organization collects gauge-theory submissions and the group's work to understand, verify, and assess them. Submissions may come from OpenAI, other organizations, or individual researchers. It provides a common place for the submitted proofs, independent checks, informal explanations, comparisons between submissions, and assessments of what the results establish.

## Proposed repository structure

We maintain **one archive repository for each submission accepted for inclusion**, preserving the received files, authorship, license notices, and source checksums. The group's additions are kept outside that original submission.

We also maintain **one results repository for each major category: YM, QCD, and chiralSU5**. It brings together relevant submissions, independent checks, informal explanations, comparisons, and assessments. Each assessment identifies the exact submission and checks it covers. Formal theorems, informal claims, and the group's conclusions are distinguished clearly.

## Results and assessments

Our results categories are **YM** (pure Yang–Mills theory), **QCD**, and **chiralSU5** (chiral SU(5) gauge theory). Each category will bring together the relevant submissions, independent checks, explanations, and assessments.

Links to submission archives, results repositories, and assessments will be added here when those materials are released. The group's published updates are available in the [statements archive](/statements/README.md).

## Making a submission

To propose a submission, email [yangmills4d@gmail.com](mailto:yangmills4d@gmail.com) with a short summary and a link to a fixed revision or dated copy of the materials. For a GitHub repository, include the commit identifier to be considered. Identify the relevant category (YM, QCD, or chiralSU5), the authors, and the results you claim. Include an explanatory document and any formal source code, together with the license or terms under which the group may host the materials.

We do not accept public issues, pull requests, or repository comments on our submission and results repositories; please email feedback and proposed contributions to [yangmills4d@gmail.com](mailto:yangmills4d@gmail.com).

Submissions must meet a minimum standard of relevance, clarity, and mathematical substance. State the problem, definitions, assumptions, and claimed results precisely enough to assess them, and provide substantive arguments supporting those claims. Distinguish formalized results from informal arguments and identify known gaps or unproved steps. For formal results, include the toolchain and dependency versions, instructions for reproducing the checks, and a description of any additional axioms or proof placeholders.

The group decides which submissions meet this standard and which to include. **The group does not promise to post or review submissions, or to reply to submitters, even when these requirements are met.** Posting a submission does not constitute endorsement or certification of its claims.
